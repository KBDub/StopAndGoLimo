## H2: A Century-Plus of South Side Baseball History

### H3: From the 39th Street Grounds to Old Comiskey Park

The White Sox have called Chicago's South Side home since the team's earliest days, with the franchise's ballpark history stretching back over a century. Charles Comiskey built his "Baseball Palace of the World" at 35th Street and Shields Avenue, opening what would become known as Comiskey Park in 1910 — a ballpark that would serve as the team's home for 81 seasons and host some of the earliest night games in Chicago baseball history.

### H3: The Move Across 35th Street to the Modern Ballpark

In 1991, the White Sox moved into a new ballpark built directly across the street from the original Comiskey Park, initially also named Comiskey Park before being renamed U.S. Cellular Field in 2003, Guaranteed Rate Field in 2016, and most recently Rate Field in December 2024. Regardless of the name on the marquee at any given moment, the ballpark has remained a fixture of the same South Side blocks for generations of Sox fans, and dirt from the original Comiskey Park infield was carried over and used in the new ballpark — a small but meaningful thread connecting old and new.

### H3: Championships and South Side Pride

The White Sox have won three World Series championships in franchise history — 1906, 1917, and 2005 — with the 2005 title breaking a long championship drought and setting off one of the most memorable celebrations in modern South Side history. The team's black, white, and silver color scheme and its South Side identity fuel one of the most storied rivalries in baseball with the North Side's Cubs, a rivalry that plays out every year in the Crosstown Classic.

## H2: Bridgeport and Armour Square — The Neighborhoods Around the Ballpark

### H3: One of Chicago's Oldest and Most Storied Neighborhoods

Bridgeport, sitting just west of Rate Field, is among the oldest and most historically significant neighborhoods in Chicago, long known as the traditional home of several Chicago mayors, including the Daley family. Once a heavily Irish, industrial neighborhood built around the stockyards and rail lines, Bridgeport today is one of the city's most ethnically diverse working-class communities, blending its historic roots with newer generations of residents.

### H3: Armour Square and the Ballpark's Immediate Blocks

Rate Field itself sits in the Armour Square community area, a compact neighborhood bordered by the Dan Ryan Expressway to the east. The area immediately around the park has a more understated, neighborhood feel compared to the North Side's Wrigleyville — a laid-back, come-for-the-baseball atmosphere that longtime Sox fans wear as a point of pride.

### H3: Local Food and Bridgeport Institutions

Bridgeport is home to longstanding neighborhood restaurants that have become part of the South Side game day ritual for generations — from family-run pizzerias with recipes dating back decades to classic Italian dining rooms serving handmade pasta. Our chauffeurs are happy to build a Bridgeport dinner stop into your game day itinerary, before or after first pitch.
