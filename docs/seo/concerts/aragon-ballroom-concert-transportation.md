## H2: The History of the Aragon Ballroom

### H3: A Spanish Palace Built to Clean Up Chicago's Dance Halls

The Aragon Ballroom opened on July 15, 1926, built by brothers Andrew and William Karzas, who had made their early fortunes in nickelodeons and movie theaters before turning to grand ballrooms. Designed with a Spanish Baroque stucco exterior by architects Huszagh and Hill, and an interior styled after the courtyard of a Moorish castle by movie-palace designer John Eberson, the Aragon was built specifically to counter the era's reputation of dance halls as disreputable venues — enforcing formal dress codes, a strict no-alcohol policy, and watchful floor staff to keep dancing respectable.

### H3: The Most Beautiful Ballroom in the World

Costing roughly $2 million to build — an enormous sum for the era — the Aragon quickly earned a reputation as one of the most elaborate entertainment venues of its time, complete with crystal chandeliers, mosaic tiles, and a painted ceiling designed to simulate a starlit Mediterranean night sky. At its peak, crowds exceeding 18,000 attended across a six-day business week, with a nightly radio broadcast carrying the ballroom's big-band sound across the country.

### H3: From Ballroom Dancing to Rock and Roll

Regular ballroom dancing at the Aragon ended in 1964 as attendance declined with the rise of television and changing entertainment habits. Since then, the historic room has been reborn as one of Chicago's most storied concert venues, hosting legendary performances from acts spanning every era and genre of popular music, all inside the same Moorish-courtyard interior that first opened in 1926.

### H4: Why This History Shapes Your Evening

Very few concert venues in America carry the Aragon's blend of architectural grandeur and musical legacy, and our chauffeurs treat every Uptown drop-off with an appreciation for exactly what makes this room special.

## H2: Uptown — One of Chicago's Great Entertainment Neighborhoods

### H3: A Historic Entertainment District Built Around One Intersection

The blocks surrounding Lawrence Avenue and Broadway in Uptown have hosted entertainment for nearly a century, anchored by the Aragon alongside the historic Riviera Theatre, the Uptown Theatre, and the legendary Green Mill jazz club — a Prohibition-era speakeasy once frequented by Al Capone.

### H3: The Green Mill and Uptown's Jazz Age Legends

Local legend holds that secret tunnels once connected the Green Mill's basement to the Aragon, a nod to the neighborhood's Prohibition-era history and its deep roots in Chicago's jazz and dance-hall culture.

#### H4: A Neighborhood on the CTA Red Line

The Aragon's location directly on the Red Line at the Lawrence stop made it easy to reach for generations of Chicagoans, and it remains one of the most transit-accessible major concert venues in the city — though for a group night out, a chauffeured ride still beats navigating the L with a crowd.
