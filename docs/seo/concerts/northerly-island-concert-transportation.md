## H2: The History of Northerly Island and Huntington Bank Pavilion

### H3: From Daniel Burnham's Vision to a Working Airport

Northerly Island was first imagined in Daniel Burnham and Edward Bennett's landmark 1909 Plan of Chicago as a natural lakefront haven. Instead, the peninsula spent decades as Meigs Field, a single-runway general aviation airport that operated from 1948 to 2003 — a use that never sat comfortably with Burnham's original vision that Chicago's lakefront should belong to the public as parkland.

### H3: The Midnight Demolition That Made Way for Music

In one of the most famous moments in modern Chicago civic history, Mayor Richard M. Daley ordered the airport's single runway bulldozed overnight on March 30-31, 2003, permanently ending Meigs Field's operation and clearing the way for the site's conversion into parkland. A concert venue followed just two years later, opening in June 2005 as the 8,000-seat Charter One Pavilion before later renaming to FirstMerit Bank Pavilion and, since 2017, Huntington Bank Pavilion.

### H3: Growing Into One of the Region's Premier Amphitheaters

A major 2013 expansion grew the venue's capacity from roughly 8,000 to as many as 30,000, adding a sloped lawn area and additional seating — transforming Northerly Island into one of the two largest outdoor amphitheaters in the Chicagoland region, alongside the Tinley Park venue.

### H4: Why This History Shapes Your Evening

Few concert venues anywhere carry a backstory quite like Northerly Island's — a lakefront park reclaimed from an airport in the middle of the night — and our chauffeurs bring an appreciation for that history to every drop-off on the peninsula.
