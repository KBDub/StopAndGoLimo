## H2: The History of Park West

### H3: From Silent-Era Movie Palace to Live Music Icon

The building that now houses Park West opened on November 25, 1916, as the Lane Court Theatre, a vaudeville and movie house built by the Ascher Brothers. Over the following decades it operated under several names, including the Town Theatre, before falling into serious disrepair by the mid-1970s — at one point so dilapidated that its own marquee had to be removed for fear it would collapse onto pedestrians below.

### H3: A Diamond-Shaped Rebirth

As Lincoln Park's fortunes rose through the 1970s, the aging theater was rescued and reborn as Park West, transformed into a live concert hall that made use of the building's rare diamond-shaped interior — a layout that gives the room unusually strong sightlines and natural acoustics unlike almost any other venue in the city.

### H3: A Stage for Music Legends

Since its transformation, Park West has hosted an extraordinary list of major artists across genres, drawing performers who would go on to headline arenas and stadiums, all within a room intimate enough to feel like a private show. The venue's reputation as one of Chicago's best-sounding rooms has made it a favorite stop for touring artists who prefer a smaller, acoustically rich setting over a large arena date.

### H4: Why This History Shapes Your Evening

Park West's blend of century-old architecture and modern sound-and-lighting technology makes it one of Chicago's most distinctive concert settings, and our chauffeurs bring the same appreciation for that character to every Lincoln Park drop-off.
