## H2: The History of Ravinia Festival

### H3: From Electric Railroad Amusement Park to America's Oldest Music Festival

Ravinia opened on August 15, 1904, not as a concert hall but as an amusement park built by the Chicago & Milwaukee Electric Railroad to attract ridership to its new rail line north of the city. The park featured a baseball diamond, an electric fountain, and a dance hall alongside its earliest musical programming, and today it holds the distinction of being the oldest outdoor music festival in North America.

### H3: The Summer Opera Capital and the Birth of the CSO Residency

By 1914, Ravinia had built a reputation as the "summer opera capital of the world," drawing celebrated European singers who crossed the Atlantic to perform at the Metropolitan Opera and stayed on in the Midwest through the summer season. The Great Depression forced Ravinia to close for several years, but in 1936 a group of concerned North Shore residents formed the Ravinia Festival Association and revived the grounds with a summer residency by the Chicago Symphony Orchestra — a relationship that continues to this day and remains the anchor of Ravinia's identity as a classical music destination.

### H3: A Uniquely Diverse Modern Lineup

Today's Ravinia season blends its classical roots with rock, pop, jazz, R&B, country, and more across three venues on its 36-acre campus: the 3,350-seat open-air Pavilion, the intimate 850-seat Martin Theatre (the only structure remaining from the original 1904 construction), and the 450-seat Bennett Gordon Hall.

### H4: Why the History Matters for Your Evening

A night at Ravinia carries more than a century of Chicagoland cultural tradition, and our chauffeurs treat every Pavilion drop-off with the same care whether you're headed to a CSO performance or a headlining summer tour stop.
