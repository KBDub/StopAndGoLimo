## H2: The History of Soldier Field

### H3: A Design Competition and a Monument to War

Soldier Field's story began in 1919, when the South Park Commission held a design competition for a new lakefront stadium in Grant Park. The winning design, by architects Holabird and Roche, featured the now-iconic Doric colonnades that still define the stadium's exterior today. Construction began in 1922, and the stadium opened on October 9, 1924, originally as Grant Park Municipal Stadium before being renamed Soldier Field on November 11, 1925, as a memorial to American soldiers who died in combat.

### H3: A Century of Legendary Moments

In its earliest configuration, Soldier Field could seat well over 100,000 spectators and hosted some of the largest crowds in American sports history, including the legendary 1927 Dempsey-Tunney boxing match. Over the following century, the stadium hosted everything from the first ski jump competition held inside a stadium to Barack Obama's 2008 election night rally, cementing its role as one of Chicago's great civic gathering places.

### H3: The Modern Renovation and a Complicated Landmark Status

Between 2002 and 2003, Soldier Field underwent a nearly $400 million renovation that inserted a modern, glass-and-steel seating bowl inside the historic colonnade shell — a striking architectural contrast that one observer famously compared to a spaceship landing inside a colosseum. The renovation modernized the fan experience significantly, but it was extensive enough that Soldier Field lost its National Historic Landmark status in 2006, one of the few landmarks ever delisted after a major renovation.

### H4: Why This History Shapes Your Ride

Soldier Field is as much a civic monument as it is a stadium, and our chauffeurs treat every drop-off along the colonnades with the same appreciation for its century of Chicago history.

## H2: The Museum Campus and Lakefront Neighborhood

### H3: A Stadium Inside a World-Class Museum Campus

Soldier Field sits at the heart of Chicago's 57-acre Museum Campus, alongside the Field Museum, the Shedd Aquarium, and the Adler Planetarium — a cluster of institutions that trace back to Daniel Burnham and Edward Bennett's 1909 Plan of Chicago and its vision for the lakefront.

### H3: The Colonnades — A Year-Round Lakefront Attraction

Soldier Field's colonnades remain open to the public year-round on non-event days, offering a free, scenic place to walk along the lakefront with sweeping views of Lake Michigan and the downtown skyline — a detail many out-of-town visitors don't know until they experience it.

#### H4: A Neighborhood Built Around the Lake, Not a Bar Scene

Unlike Wrigleyville or the West Loop, the immediate Soldier Field area is defined by parkland and lakefront views rather than bars and restaurants, which makes coordinating a broader evening — dinner downtown, the event itself, a nightcap in the South Loop — especially valuable with a professional chauffeur handling the logistics.
