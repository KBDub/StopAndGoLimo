## H2: The History of the United Center and Its Predecessor

### H3: The Original Madhouse on Madison

Before the United Center existed, Chicago Stadium stood on the same stretch of Madison Street from 1929 until its demolition in the mid-1990s. Known as the original "Madhouse on Madison" for its deafening acoustics and famously raucous crowds, Chicago Stadium hosted the Bulls, the Blackhawks, national political conventions, and even an NFL playoff game before giving way to a new era of Chicago sports and entertainment.

### H3: The Birth of the United Center

The United Center was built through a partnership between Blackhawks owner Bill Wirtz and Bulls chairman Jerry Reinsdorf, opening its doors on August 18, 1994, directly across the street from the site of the old stadium. At roughly 960,000 square feet, it remains one of the largest arenas in the country, with a concert capacity of up to 23,500 — the largest arena by capacity in the NBA.

### H3: The House That Jordan Built

The United Center's rise coincided with the Bulls' championship dynasty of the 1990s, cementing its nickname as "The House That Jordan Built." A towering statue of Michael Jordan greets visitors outside the arena, alongside tributes to Blackhawks legends Bobby Hull and Stan Mikita — a physical reminder of just how much championship history has passed through these doors.

Whether you're headed to a Bulls game, a Blackhawks matchup, or a global concert tour, our chauffeurs understand that the United Center carries three decades of Chicago sports and entertainment history — and they treat every drop-off with the attention that history deserves.
